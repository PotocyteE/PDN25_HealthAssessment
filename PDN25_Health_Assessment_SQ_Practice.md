## L3: Nutritional and Anthropometric Assessment

### 1. BMI Calculation (WHO Standard)
A client weighs 75 kg and is 1.70 m tall. Calculate the BMI and classify according to WHO standards.

**Model Answer:**
BMI = weight (kg) / [height (m)]² = 75 / (1.70)² = 75 / 2.89 ≈ 25.95 kg/m²  
Classification: Overweight (25 – 29.9)

WHO Adult Classification:
- < 18.5: Underweight
- 18.5 – 24.9: Normal weight
- 25 – 29.9: Overweight
- 30 – 39.9: Obesity
- ≥ 40: Extreme obesity

Note: Do not apply to clients with oedema or athletes.

### 2. Waist-to-Hip Ratio
Explain how to measure waist and hip circumference and interpret the ratio. What does a high ratio indicate?

**Model Answer:**
Waist circumference: measured just above the iliac crests.  
Hip circumference: measured at the largest circumference of the buttocks.  
Ratio = Waist / Hip  

Android obesity (upper body fat): ≥ 1.0 in men or ≥ 0.8 in women → higher health risk.  
Gynoid obesity: fat mainly in hips and thighs.

## L4_1: Head and Neck – Thyroid Gland

### Inspect and Palpate the Neck for Thyroid Gland
Describe the steps for inspection and palpation of the thyroid gland (posterior and anterior approaches). What are normal vs abnormal findings?

**Model Answer:**
**Inspection:**
- Position standing lamp to shine tangentially across the neck to highlight swelling.
- Tilt head back to stretch skin.
- Inspect as person swallows (thyroid moves up then falls; may need sip of water).

**Posterior Approach:**
- Move behind person.
- Ask to sit up straight, bend head slightly forward and to the right (relaxes right neck muscles).
- Use left hand to push trachea slightly to the right.
- Curve right fingers between trachea and sternomastoid muscle, retract slightly.
- Ask person to take sip of water; thyroid moves up under fingers.
- Reverse for left side.
- Normal: often cannot palpate normal adult thyroid. Isthmus may be felt over tracheal rings in long thin neck. Lateral lobes usually not palpable; if palpable, rubbery but smooth.
- Abnormal: enlarged lobes easily palpated before swallowing, tender, nodules or lumps.

**Anterior Approach:**
- Stand facing person.
- Identify isthmus by placing thumb 3 cm below thyroid cartilage prominence as person swallows.
- Ask to tip head forward and to the right.
- Use right thumb to displace trachea slightly to person's right.
- Hook left thumb and fingers around sternomastoid muscle; feel for lobe enlargement as person swallows.
- If enlarged, auscultate for bruit (soft, pulsatile, whooshing sound with bell of stethoscope). Bruit indicates hyperplasia (e.g., hyperthyroidism). Not normally present.

## L4_2: Eyes – Confrontation Test

### Testing Visual Fields (Confrontation Test)
Describe the procedure for the Confrontation test and normal findings.

**Model Answer:**
Screens for loss of peripheral vision. Compares person's peripheral vision with examiner's (assuming examiner's is normal).

Steps:
1. Position yourself at eye level about 2 feet away, looking straight at client.
2. Ask client to cover one eye with opaque card; you cover the opposite eye.
3. Test the uncovered eye of client.
4. Hold a wiggling finger as target midline between you and client.
5. Slowly advance the wiggling finger in from the periphery in several directions.
6. Ask client to say “now” when target is first seen (should be at same time as you see it).
7. For temporal direction, start finger somewhat behind the person.
8. Estimate the angle.

Normal results: about 50° upward, 90° temporally, 70° inferiorly, 60° nasally.

Abnormal: person unable to see object as examiner does → peripheral field loss (screens for glaucoma in older adults; acutely diminished with retinal disease or stroke).

## L4_3: Ears – Whispered Voice Test

### Whispered Voice Test
Describe the procedure and how to interpret results.

**Model Answer:**
1. Stand arm’s length (2 feet) behind the person.
2. Test one ear at a time while masking hearing in the other ear (place one finger on the tragus and push it in and out of the auditory meatus).
3. Move head to 1 to 2 feet from the person’s ear.
4. Exhale fully and whisper slowly a set of 3 random numbers and letters (e.g., “5, B, 6”).
5. If response incorrect, repeat with different combination of 3.
6. Passing score: correct repetition of 4 of a possible 6 numbers/letters.
7. Assess other ear with another set (e.g., “4, K, 2”).

Normal: client repeats each number/letter correctly.  
Abnormal: unable to repeat 3 of the 6 items (detects high-tone loss).

## L5: Respiratory System – Collection of Objective Data

### Inspection, Palpation, Percussion, Auscultation (key points)
Summarise normal findings for each technique in respiratory assessment.

**Model Answer (key points from notes):**
- **Inspection:** Normal relaxed posture, regular even respiration, skin colour consistent with genetic background. Abnormal: tripod position, nasal flaring, cyanosis/pallor, barrel chest, pectus excavatum.
- **Palpation:** (Confirm expansion, tactile fremitus – normal symmetric).
- **Percussion:** Resonance over lung fields (low-pitched, clear, hollow). Abnormal: asymmetry, dullness (consolidation), hyperresonance (emphysema/pneumothorax).
- **Auscultation:** Normal vesicular breath sounds. Abnormal: crackles, wheezes, etc.

## L6: Cardiovascular System

### 1. Health History Taking on CVS (PQRST for Chest Pain)
What questions would you ask using PQRST for a client with chest pain?

**Model Answer:**
- **P (Provocation/Palliation):** What brings the pain? What makes it worse/better?
- **Q (Quality):** How is the nature? What does it feel like?
- **R (Region/Radiation):** Where is the pain? Does it radiate?
- **S (Severity):** How severe is the pain? Rate 0-10.
- **T (Timing):** When does the pain occur? Onset, duration, frequency.

Also assess dyspnoea, orthopnoea, cough, fatigue, cyanosis/pallor, oedema, nocturia, past/family cardiac history, risk factors.

### 2. Carotid Artery – Palpation and Auscultation
Describe technique and normal findings.

**Model Answer:**
**Palpation:**
- Palpate each carotid artery medial to sternomastoid gently.
- Avoid excessive pressure on carotid sinus (could slow HR via vagal stimulation, especially older adults).
- Palpate only one at a time (avoid compromising cerebral blood flow).
- Feel contour and amplitude.
- Normal: smooth contour with brisk upstroke/slower downstroke, strength 2+ (moderate), regular, bilateral equal.
- Amplitude: 2+ normal; 3+ bounding; 1+ weak; 0 absent.

**Auscultation:**
- For older people or those with CVD symptoms, auscultate for bruit.
- Keep neck neutral.
- Ask client to take a breath, exhale, and hold briefly (so tracheal sounds don’t mask).
- Lightly apply bell of stethoscope at 3 levels: angle of jaw, midcervical, base of neck.
- Avoid compressing artery.
- Normal: no bruit.
- Abnormal: bruit = turbulence, marker for atherosclerotic disease.

### 3. Jugular Vein Inspection and Estimate JVP
Describe inspection and measurement of JVP.

**Model Answer:**
**Inspection:**
- Stand on person’s right side (veins have direct route to heart).
- Position client supine at 30–45° angle (wherever top of vein/pulsations best seen).
- Remove pillow (avoid flexing neck); head in same plane as trunk.
- Turn head slightly away from examined side.
- Direct strong light tangentially onto neck.
- Note external jugular vein overlying sternomastoid; internal jugular pulsations in suprasternal notch or around clavicle.
- Normal: pulsations of internal jugular veins visible.
- Abnormal: unilateral distention (local cause); full distended external jugulars above 45° = increased CVP (heart failure).

**Estimate JVP:**
1. Use angle of Louis (sternal angle) as reference; compare with highest level of venous pulsation.
2. Hold vertical ruler on sternal angle.
3. Align straight edge (T-square) to level of pulsation.
4. Read level of intersection on vertical ruler.
5. Document position, e.g., “internal jugular vein pulsations 3 cm above sternal angle when elevated 30 degrees”.
- Normal: < 3 cm above sternal angle.
- Abnormal: > 3 cm at 45° (heart failure, tamponade, constrictive pericarditis).

## L7: Abdomen

### 1. Umbilicus (Cullen Sign)
Normal vs abnormal findings, including Cullen sign.

**Model Answer:**
Normal: midline and inverted, no discolouration, inflammation or hernia.  
Abnormal: everted (ascites/mass), deeply sunken (obesity), enlarged and everted (umbilical hernia), bluish periumbilical colour → intraperitoneal bleeding (Cullen sign).

### 2. Costovertebral Angle Tenderness
How to test and interpretation.

**Model Answer:**
Normal: client feels a thud but no pain.  
Abnormal: sharp pain = inflammation of kidney or perinephric area (e.g., pyelonephritis, renal calculi).

### 3. Fluid Wave Test
Describe method and interpretation.

**Model Answer:**
Differentiates ascites from gaseous distention.  
Method: Stand on client’s right side. Client places ulnar edge of hand firmly on midline abdomen. Place left hand on client’s right flank. With right hand, reach across and give client’s left flank a firm strike.  
If ascites present: feel distinct tap on left hand.  
If gas/adipose: no change.

### 4. Shifting Dullness
Describe method.

**Model Answer:**
Client supine: percuss midline (tympany), then down side until dullness (mark). Turn to right side; percuss upper side downward – expect tympany superior, dullness lower. Shifting level of dullness indicates fluid. (USG is definitive.)

### 5. Palpation of Liver (Murphy’s Sign)
Describe liver palpation and Murphy’s sign.

**Model Answer:**
Method: Left hand under back parallel to 11th/12th ribs, lift up. Right hand on RUQ, fingers parallel to midline. Push deeply down and under right costal margin. Client breathes slowly; with every exhalation move hand up 1–2 cm.  
Normal: often not palpable, or edge feels firm regular ridge, no pain.  
Abnormal: Murphy’s sign (inspiratory arrest) – in acute cholecystitis, descending liver pushes inflamed gallbladder onto hand → sharp pain, client stops inspiration midway.

### 6. Rebound Tenderness
Describe and interpretation.

**Model Answer:**
Assess when client reports pain. Push slowly and deeply then lift up quickly.  
Normal: no pain on release.  
Abnormal: sharp stabbing pain on release → rebound tenderness (reliable sign of peritoneal inflammation).

## L9: Musculoskeletal System

### 1. Wrists and Hands – Phalen Test and Tinel Sign (Carpal Tunnel)
Describe both tests.

**Model Answer:**
**Phalen Test:** Hold both hands back to back while flexing wrists 90° for 60 seconds.  
Normal: no symptoms.  
Abnormal: numbness and burning → positive Phalen = carpal tunnel syndrome.

**Tinel Sign:** Direct percussion of the median nerve location at the wrist.  
Normal: no symptoms.  
Abnormal: burning and tingling → positive Tinel = carpal tunnel syndrome.

### 2. Knees – Tests for Knee Effusion (Bulge Sign & Ballottement)
Describe both.

**Model Answer:**
**Bulge Sign** (small amounts 4–8 ml): Firmly stroke up on medial aspect of knee 2–3 times to displace fluid. Tap lateral aspect and watch for fluid wave in hollow of medial side.  
Normal: none present.

**Ballottement Test** (larger amounts): One hand compresses suprapatellar pouch; other hand pushes patella sharply against femur.  
Normal: no sound if fluid not present.

### 3. Spine – Straight Leg Raising or Lasègue Test
Describe procedure and interpretation.

**Model Answer:**
To confirm sciatica and herniated nucleus pulposus.  
Keep knee extended, raise affected leg just short of point of pain, then dorsiflex foot. Raise unaffected leg while other flat; inquire about involved side.  
Normal: no pain on raising leg.  
Abnormal: presents/worsens sciatic pain, resists elevation. Positive: increased pain on dorsiflexion of foot → strongly suggests herniated disc.

### 4. Measure Leg Length Discrepancy
True vs apparent leg length.

**Model Answer:**
**True leg length:** From anterior iliac crest to medial malleolus (crossing medial side of knee). Normal: equal or within 1 cm.  
**Apparent leg length:** From umbilicus (nonfixed) to medial malleolus.  
If true lengths equal/within 1 cm but apparent unequal → pelvic obliquity or adduction/flexion deformity of hip.
